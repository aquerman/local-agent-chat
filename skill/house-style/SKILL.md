# Document house style

Apply these rules when drafting or editing a document. If the user's own instructions
conflict with them, the user wins.

## Voice

- Plain, direct and friendly. Write like a capable colleague, not a brochure.
- Active voice, present tense where possible. "We ship on Friday", not "It is planned that...".
- Short sentences (aim under 25 words). One idea per paragraph.
- No filler openers ("In today's fast-paced world"), no hype words ("revolutionary",
  "seamless", "leverage"), no closing summaries that repeat the text.

## Structure

- Lead with the point: the answer, decision or ask goes in the first two sentences.
- Headings in sentence case ("Next steps", not "Next Steps"). No more than three levels.
- Use bullets for lists of three or more parallel items; use prose for reasoning.
- Put numbers, dates and owners where they can be scanned: lists or tables.

## Formatting

- Dates as `20 October 2026` in prose, `2026-10-20` in tables and file names.
- Numbers: words for one to nine, digits for 10 and up; always digits with units (`5 GB`).
- Bold sparingly, for the one phrase a skimmer must not miss. No italics for emphasis.
- Links on descriptive text ("see the release notes"), never "click here".

## Before you finish

Re-read once and cut anything that does not help the reader act or understand. A shorter
document that says the same thing is better.
